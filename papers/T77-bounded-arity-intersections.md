# Bounded arity and intersections of generating sets in universal algebras

**Draft research note v0.1 — 24 November 2026.** Authorship and contributions to be confirmed. Internal mathematical development, not submitted or externally refereed. Historical priority unresolved.

Let S be a nonempty algebra with total operations of positive finite arity at most d, for an integer d≥1. Write D for the set of indispensable elements, equivalently the intersection of all generating sets or the set of x for which S\{x} is closed. Generation uses finite terms only, with no nullary operations.

**Theorem A.** If S is finitely generated, D is the intersection of at most d+1 inclusion-minimal generating sets. More precisely the bound is min(d+1,r−|D|+1), where r is its least generating-set cardinality.

Proof. Any generating set contains a finite generating subset: express each member of a fixed finite generating set by a finite term in the given set, and take the union of the finitely many leaves. Shrink to an inclusion-minimal set. Choose a finite inclusion-minimal generating set A of size r. For each x∈A\D choose a basic-operation witness yielding x with all arguments different from x. At least one argument lies outside A, else A is redundant. Draw edges from x to all witness arguments lying in A\D, giving outdegree at most d−1. Order A\D and greedily assign d colours, excluding colours of previously coloured out-neighbours. Every monochromatic edge points forward in the order. For each colour class C, S\C generates S by recovering the omitted vertices in reverse order using their witnesses. Intersect the d complements with A and shrink each to an inclusion-minimal generating set: the intersection is D, since no generator can omit D. The bound r−|D|+1 also follows by choosing a generating set omitting each x∈A\D. □

**Theorem B (ZFC).** Without any hypothesis of finite generation, D is the intersection of at most d+1 generating sets, not necessarily inclusion-minimal. If every generating set contains an inclusion-minimal generating subset, the same bound holds for inclusion-minimal sets.

Proof. The following directed-graph lemma suffices: a loopless digraph of maximum outdegree d admits a colouring with d+1 colours with no infinite monochromatic directed walk. Well-order the vertices and successively take all vertices forward-reachable from the next uncovered vertex, omitting previously covered vertices. Each resulting block is countable and edges point within the block or to earlier blocks. Within each block choose distinct real priorities with no infinite strictly monotone directed path in either direction. Such priorities exist by independent continuous random priorities: for a fixed initial vertex the probability of a monotone simple path of length m is at most 2d^m/(m+1)!, tending to zero. Take a countable union over initial vertices. Colour the blocks successively; within a block colour each vertex after its lower-priority out-neighbours (well-founded recursion) and exclude their colours and those of out-neighbours in earlier blocks. At most d colours are excluded. A monochromatic edge can only point within the same block to a higher priority, so an infinite monochromatic walk is impossible.

Apply the lemma to S\D with an edge from each x to each nonindispensable argument of a chosen basic-operation witness for x excluding x. For each colour class C, S\C generates S: the monochromatic dependency tree from an omitted x is finitely branching with no infinite branch, hence finite by König's infinity lemma. Evaluate witnesses backwards from its leaves, which are available from the complement. The complements intersect in D. Shrinking is valid under the stated refinement hypothesis. □

**Sharpness.** On d+1 elements define one d-ary operation sending d distinct arguments to the unique missing element, and otherwise returning its first argument. The inclusion-minimal generating sets are precisely the d-subsets. Every generating set omits at most one element and D is empty, so d+1 sets are necessary. This also proves sharpness for arbitrary generating sets.

**Remarks.** For d=2 the bound three needs neither associativity nor a finite carrier, generalising the finite-semigroup theorem previously proved by Green/Rees methods. For finite semigroups the exact classification by unsupported Klein-four J-classes (Study 20) remains a distinct structural question. The previous bounded-depth result is not evidence that associativity causes the bound. The Prüfer group has no inclusion-minimal generating set; adjoining a new identity yields a monoid with nonempty D and still no such set, although its ordinary generating-set depth is two. With unbounded finite arities even a countable depth can be necessary (Study 21, T82).

**Priority.** The irreducible-element notion is classical: Berman and Bordalo, Discrete Mathematics 245 (2002), 63–79, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00364-8. The above short proof uses elementary finite-term generation and greedy colouring; the graph lemma uses standard probabilistic and well-founded methods. A preliminary literature search has not established historical novelty of either theorem or the graph lemma. A targeted expert review of universal algebra, closure operators and infinite directed graph colouring is required before submission. The claims concern positive-arity total operations and finite-term closure, not arbitrary closure operators, infinitary operations or nullary constants.

## Addendum (Study 22): finite repair and existence of irredundant bases
In a finitary closure space possessing an irredundant generating set A, any generating set differing from A by finitely many elements contains an irredundant generating subset differing finitely from A: the added elements depend on a finite support D in A, so all old generators outside the removed elements and D remain necessary; only finitely many elements require pruning. Consequently every finite set avoidable by an ordinary generating set can be avoided by such a finite modification of A. The indispensable set is the intersection of all irredundant generating sets as soon as one exists, without assuming refinement. If A\D(S) is finite the sharp d+1 bound also follows by finite repair after the finite colouring in Theorem A. This does not settle a finite bound on irredundant intersections when that difference is infinite and refinement fails. An explicit torsion abelian example of failure of refinement is the direct sum of cyclic p-groups of unbounded orders (Study 22); the bounded-primary classification is classical (Dlab 1961), not claimed as new. [Proofs and limits](../docs/32-experiment-22-finite-repair-and-refinement.md).

## Addendum (Study 23): a sharp failure for irredundant bases
A single commutative, nonassociative binary operation on a countable carrier has irredundant bases, ordinary generating-set depth two, and irredundant depth aleph_0. Let a_i represent singleton i, b_i (i≥2) represent the prefix [1,i], and b_1=a_1. Use symmetric rules b_i*b_i=b_(i−1), b_(i−1)*b_i=a_i, b_(i−1)*a_i=b_i, and otherwise the minimum in an order placing the a_i before the b_i. Generated elements are exactly those with support contained in the union of seed supports. All irredundant generating sets are the entire singleton family or {b_i} with every a_j for j>i. Every finite intersection contains an infinite singleton tail, although the full intersection is empty. The singleton family and the prefix family are disjoint ordinary generating sets. Thus neither existence of a basis nor commutativity substitutes for refinement in the irredundant bound. This is not associative. In contrast, any infinite group possessing a semigroup-irredundant generating set has a disjoint one (a finite translation repair argument), and cancellative commutative monoids with a basis have equal depths at most three. The unrestricted infinite associative case remains open in this project. [Full proofs](../docs/33-experiment-23-infinite-depth-separation.md).

## Addendum (Study 24): further associative cases
For an irredundant semigroup generating set A, any witness xy=a for a∈A outside the indispensable set must have at least one factor in the J-class of a. In particular a J-trivial semigroup possessing a basis has a unique one. A strict-upper periodic translation condition yields two irredundant sets by a forest colouring; it applies to commutative semigroups with finite nonregular J-classes and singleton regular J-classes. For arbitrary strong semilattices of groups of order at most two, the two depths coincide and are at most two whenever a basis exists, even without reverse well-foundedness. An infinite ladder of C2-groups has a basis and fails refinement, yet both depths are two. The general infinite associative question remains unresolved.
