# One-third universal bound and joint-statistic omission of sink queries
24/11/2026. Internal mathematical note following [two-sided SCC geometry](P03-two-sided-scc-upper-bound-2026-11-24.md) and [general-ideal statistics](P03-general-ideal-statistics-2026-11-24.md). No external referee, Lean certificate or historical priority claim. No new T numbers or main modification.

## 1. Setting and result
Use exactly the previous D* model: finite labelled monoid M, designated proper nonempty ideal K, known quotient M/K, all products involving K, unit actions, labelled principal ideals and internal tables of proper principal ideals. Additional side information includes r, H and g_r,...,g_(r+L) from T56. Generation uses nonempty words, computation is free, a multiplication query returns one product. Let A=M\(G∪K), a=|A|, N=a(a−1), and J_L={j:1≤j≤L, j=1 or j even}, d=1+|J_L|.
**Theorem A.** Without using statistics, emptiness of E has deterministic query upper bound floor(N/3).
**Theorem B.** With the prescribed statistics, emptiness has deterministic query upper bound
\[
\max\{0,\lfloor a(a-1)/3\rfloor-d\}.
\]
In the branch where no known exterior or regular-interior indispensable element exists, the same algorithm determines the exact cardinality of E (if exterior E is nonempty, the emptiness decision stops without queries, but the cardinality statement need not apply). More precisely, if q is the number of sink SCCs of the graph for all ambiguous missing nonregular classes, at most max(0,q−d) queries suffice. These are upper bounds, not asserted sharp. The reduction of the leading coefficient to 1/3 comes from geometry alone. Statistics save an additional d queries in the worst-case bound, not a further asymptotic coefficient for fixed L.

## 2. Extended block charging
As before, if exterior E is nonempty, decide immediately. Otherwise every exterior nonunit has a known nonself binary factorization. Take any of the closed witness-cell sets U from the preceding notes (singleton targets or selected ambiguous nonregular class first-entry products), and its full two-sided witness-induced dependency graph. Every vertex has an outgoing edge and none has a loop. Every sink SCC is recoverable from one queried representative; all cells reach a sink. Let s count two-cell sinks and h count sinks of size at least three. Write X,Y for all first and second coordinates of two-cell sinks, and x=|X|, y=|Y|. Recall the previous proof that X∩Y=∅. For each two-cell sink {(u,v),(u',v')}, the first coordinates are R-equivalent, the second L-equivalent, and EVERY nonself factorization of u has first factor u', EVERY nonself factorization of v has second factor v', with the symmetric statements for the partners. These conditions define fixed-point-free involutions α on X and β on Y, independent of which sink is chosen (uniqueness of the factor and existence of a nonself factorization prove well-definedness). In particular u=α(u)c and α(u)=uc' for suitable nonself factors, and v=bβ(v), β(v)=b'v.
For any relevant cell with second coordinate u∈X, its partner with second coordinate α(u) and unchanged first coordinate is linked by two opposite right-induced edges: both products lie in K by R-equivalence and the ideal property. Similarly for a cell with first coordinate v∈Y, changing it to β(v) gives opposite left-induced edges. Closure of U holds for the entire graph as in the previous notes.
**Lemma.** Any sink containing a cell of X×X, Y×Y or Y×X has at least four cells.
Proof: for Y×X both partner operations give four distinct mutually reachable cells. For X×X let (u,v) be in such a sink. It also contains (u,α(v)). It cannot have size two because second coordinates of two-cell sinks belong to Y, disjoint from X. Assume it has size three. Apply the left decomposition u=α(u)c to (u,v). If cv∈K, the edge leads to (c,v) with c≠u, which also has partner (c,α(v)), making four. Otherwise the edge leads to (α(u),w) with w=cv outside K. This must be the third cell. If w∈X its partner with second coordinate α(w) gives a fourth cell, so w∉X. Now apply the left decomposition α(u)=u c' to this third cell. If c'w∈K the edge goes to (c',w), distinct from all three because c'≠α(u) and w∉X. Otherwise it goes to (u,c'w), so c'w must be v or α(v). But w differs from both. Also c' differs from both, because uc'=α(u) is outside K whereas uv and uα(v) lie in K. This would be a nonself factorization of v (or α(v)) whose first factor is not its unique partner in X, contradiction. Thus no such three-cell sink exists. Exchange left and right (pass to the opposite monoid) for Y×Y. QED.
There are x(x−1)+y(y−1)+xy ordered off-diagonal cells in the three charged blocks. Every such cell either is outside all sinks (including outside U) or belongs to a sink of size l≥4. With W=N−2s−3h, a cell outside all sinks contributes one to W, and a sink of size l≥4 contributes l−3≥l/4, enough to charge one quarter per charged cell. Hence
\[
W\ge [x(x-1)+y(y-1)+xy]/4,\quad s\le xy/2.
\]
If s=0, 3h≤N directly. Otherwise x,y≥2 and x²+y²−xy−x−y=(x−y)²+(x−1)(y−1)−1≥0. Thus
\[
3(s+h)=N+s-W\le N-(x^2+y^2-xy-x-y)/4\le N.
\]
Querying one representative of each sink proves Theorem A. This strengthens 17/48 to 1/3 and retains the instance-specific estimate above. A sharper bound could still follow from further constraints on sinks of size three. No claim that all sinks have at least four cells. This argument uses both left and right decompositions and the known ideal products; it is not a count of arbitrary table cells.

## 3. Sink representatives as class-presence observations
Apply the graph to the union of first-entry witness cells of ALL ambiguous missing nonregular J-classes inside K (previous general-ideal note, Section 4). For each such class C, let it be present when a product of two strictly higher elements hits C. In a compatible table, any first-entry witness for C must be an unknown cell from A×A, since a fixed witness would make C present in every table and thus not ambiguous. Conversely a cell in A×A with output in C is a first-entry witness (both factors outside K and hence strictly higher if their product is in C). 
Along each dependency edge from a cell hitting C to its provider, the provider also hits C in that same table. The identity-output case is immediate; for a translation map, if the provider output were strictly above C, the known product involving K would be a fixed first-entry witness, impossible for an ambiguous class. This is the closure proof of the previous note, now applied to the actual output, not only possible outputs. Along paths the label C therefore propagates forward. Every cell reaches a chosen sink representative. Thus C is present iff at least one sink representative outputs a member of C. One representative can report at most one ambiguous class, or none.
Known contributions to missing classes are subtracted beforehand. The common statistics determine the number b of missing ambiguous classes, product P of their sizes, and sums of powers of their sizes at orders J_L. Since the full available multiset of ambiguous class sizes is known, the same count, product and power sums for the present classes are fixed. The quantity of interest is the number of singleton missing classes, computed as total ambiguous singleton classes minus singleton present classes, plus any fixed contribution. This is a joint observation model, not necessarily reconstruction of every table entry.

## 4. Small equal-cardinality moment uniqueness
**Lemma.** Two multisets of k positive real numbers with equal products and equal sums of j-th powers for every j∈J_L, where k≤d=1+|J_L|, are identical including multiplicity.
Proof: cancel common entries, leaving two multisets of the same size k'≤k. If they differ, write F(t) for the difference of the counting distribution functions (counts ≤t). It is an integer-valued compactly supported step function on the positive axis, nonzero on at least one interval. It has at most k'−1 sign changes after ignoring intervals where it vanishes: total variation is ≤2k', while each of s+1 successive nonzero sign runs forces total variation ≥2(s+1) (start and end at zero, sign crossings have magnitude at least two). Equal products give equal sums of log of entries; integration by parts for the finite step function gives ∫F(t)/t dt=0. Equal power sums similarly give ∫F(t)t^(j−1) dt=0 for j∈J_L.
Choose one positive separating point for each of the s sign changes (at the crossing or in a zero gap). A nonzero polynomial combination P of the first s+1 monomials with exponents in {0}∪J_L, in increasing order, can vanish at these s points by homogeneous linear algebra. These monomials are linearly independent. By Descartes' rule it has at most s positive roots counted with multiplicity, hence precisely those s roots, all simple. Consequently P(t)/t has alternating signs at exactly the separating points. Multiply by −1 if needed to make F(t)P(t)/t nonnegative everywhere except finitely many jump points, and strictly positive on a nonzero interval. But P(t)/t is a linear combination of the orthogonal functions above, contradiction. QED.
No claim that d is optimal for all integer supports. At L=0, d=1 and collision {1,4} versus {2,2} at k=2. At L=1, d=2 and collision {1,6,6} versus {2,2,9} at k=3. Both include singleton discrepancy.

## 5. Joint omission theorem and zero-query corollaries
Let q denote number of sink representatives in Section 3. Query any max(0,q−d) of them. For any two compatible tables with the same replies, all observed class labels are common to their present sets. Their present sets differ in at most d classes on each side, because each unqueried representative reports at most one class. Their counts are equal, so after canceling common classes the two sides have the same cardinality k≤d. They also have the same product and prescribed power sums of class sizes after cancellation. Section 4 forces identical size multisets on the two sides. In particular the singleton present counts agree; so the singleton missing count, and the desired decision, are forced. This holds for ANY choice of omitted representatives of size at most d. The algorithm may enumerate compatible completions to read the determined answer (no time bound). Combine with q≤floor(N/3) to prove Theorem B.
Further: if either the total count of present ambiguous classes or the total count of missing ambiguous classes is ≤d, their full size multiset is already determined by the common signature, so no oracle query is necessary for singleton count. This is a cardinality criterion, complementary to the distinct-size-support criterion of the previous note. The algorithm does not recover the identities of all classes: equal sizes may be exchanged. Also can omit more than d representatives whenever the compatible class-presence sets subject to the observations all have equal singleton count, as in the 18-table T54 example below. No improvement below leading coefficient 1/3 for fixed L established through this omission argument.
For L=0, the previous lower coefficient 1/32 remains, so the ratio of upper to lower leading bounds is 32/3≈10.667, compared to 34/3 previously. Avoid assuming existence of a limit of normalized maxima; lower applies along an infinite sequence, upper to all sizes. Equivalently these bound the limsup. Global optimal coefficient remains open.
