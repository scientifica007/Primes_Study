# Three-cell sinks after Green saturation: a universal 5/16 coefficient
24/11/2026. Internal mathematical note after [one-third bound](P03-one-third-joint-sink-bound-2026-11-24.md). No external referee, Lean certificate, or claim of historical priority. No T number assigned; no main modification.

## 1. Model and theorem
Retain D*, the compatible family F (possibly restricted by T56 statistics), positive-word generation, A=M outside G and K, a=|A|, N=a(a−1), and the closed witness-cell set U of the preceding notes. For the statistical algorithm use the union of first-entry witness cells for all ambiguous nonregular classes inside K. If exterior E is nonempty decide immediately. The full nonself-factorization graph of the preceding notes has left edges changing the first coordinate and right edges changing the second coordinate. A variable cell must have two J-incomparable coordinates. Use the same arrow convention (dependent to provider). Define L_x,R_x here as Green classes when used in cardinalities; translations of K are written explicitly.
**Theorem.** The augmented graph defined below has q sink SCCs satisfying
\[
4q\le |U|+2s_2+s_3\le N+|X||Y|-2s_3,
\]
where s_i counts i-cell sinks,
X={x in A: |R_x|≥2, |L_x|=1}, Y={y in A: |L_y|≥2, |R_y|=1}.
Consequently q≤floor((a(a−1)+floor(a²/4))/4). With the T56 statistics and d=1+|J_L|, emptiness costs at most
\[
\max\{0,\lfloor(a(a-1)+\lfloor a^2/4\rfloor)/4\rfloor-d\}.
\]
Take the minimum with the previous one-third bound, actual q−d and other applicable instance bounds; the new expression need not be better for small a. The leading upper coefficient is 5/16, not asserted sharp. Without statistics the same geometric bound applies (without subtracting d).

## 2. Green saturation
If x L x', choose known multipliers u,v with x=u x', x'=v x. They exist and are known from D* since the results lie outside K. For a relevant (x,y), x'y∈K and xy=u(x'y), x'y=v(xy). Thus add edges in both directions with known translations. Similarly if y R y', add both edges between (x,y),(x,y') using right multiplication on K. These edges do not require nonself decompositions. They preserve the actual output J-class (the two outputs divide each other) and hence U and ambiguity. For singleton targets this also preserves the target itself when it occurs: if t is indispensable in another table no fixed outside translation can map a different K element to t. No loops are needed. Each (x,y) in U now lies in an SCC containing the entire rectangle L_x × R_y. A sink includes all that rectangle. All classes are contained in A for elements of A. The original left/right nonself edges are retained. Querying one representative per sink still recovers every cell along dependency paths. The class-presence representative observation and omission theorem of the preceding note remain valid for these added edges.

Use the classical finite stability facts x≤_R y and x J y imply x R y, and dually for L. Also within a J-class all R-classes have equal cardinality and all L-classes have equal cardinality (finite D=J, Green's lemma). These are standard results, not new claims.

## 3. Small-sink geometry in the augmented graph
**Lemma.** In every two- or three-cell sink C, all first coordinates are R-equivalent and have singleton L-class; all second coordinates are L-equivalent and have singleton R-class. Each projection has at least two distinct elements.
Proof: every cell has a nonself left edge changing its first coordinate and a nonself right edge changing its second coordinate, so both projection sizes are at least two. A two-cell sink has two distinct coordinates in each projection. For such a sink and for the three-cell case with three distinct rows and columns, all original edges change both coordinates. They are therefore the non-K transfer edges. On the finite poset of pairs (R-class of first coordinate, L-class of second coordinate) ordered by the R-preorder and REVERSE L-preorder, every original left edge is weakly increasing and every original right edge weakly decreasing: x=uv, vy=w gives (x,y)→(u,w), x≤_R u and w≤_L y; y=rs, xr=w gives (x,y)→(w,s), w≤_R x and y≤_L s. A maximal attained pair must occur at least twice (its left edge stays at the same pair); a minimal attained pair must occur at least twice (its right edge stays there). If there is more than one attained pair, choose distinct maximal/minimal attained pairs, which would require at least four cells. Hence all pairs equal. Green saturation and the injective projections force the L-classes of first coordinates and R-classes of second coordinates to be singleton.

For projection sizes (2,2) with three cells, the shape is an L with one missing corner. The two rows have the same J-class: each has a left edge to the other row, and the first coordinate of a left edge is a factor of the original first coordinate and hence weakly higher in J. Symmetrically the two columns are J-equivalent. If either row has a nonsingleton L-class, saturation forces the two rows to share that class and fill the missing corner, impossible; similarly each column has singleton R-class. Any left edge of the K-translation type would make its two first coordinates L-related by finite stability (they are J-equivalent), impossible. Thus left edges are non-K transfers, giving mutual R-comparability of the rows. Right edges similarly give mutual L-comparability of the columns.

For projection sizes (2,3) write C={(x,y),(x,z),(w,t)} with y,z,t distinct and x≠w. All original left edges change both coordinates because no two cells share a column. In particular the edges from the x row go to (w,t), and the edge from (w,t) goes to an x row cell, which we may name (x,y). Thus x R w, t≤_L y,z and y≤_L t. The right edge from (x,z) changes second coordinate to y or t; its new second coordinate is a factor of z, so z≤_J y or t. Since y L t and t≤_L z, z J t and by stability z L t. All columns are therefore L-equivalent. The first coordinates have singleton L-classes by the no-repeated-column condition and saturation. The row w contains just one cell, so t has singleton R-class. All columns are in one J-class and all R-classes there have equal size, so y,z also have singleton R-class. Projection sizes (3,2) follow by passing to the opposite monoid. This exhausts the possibilities and proves the lemma.

In particular small sinks lie entirely in X×Y, X∩Y=∅. This is a necessary classification of retained sinks in the augmented graph, NOT a claim that all four projection shapes occur or a classification of all sinks of the original graph. The injective-projection argument already holds in the original graph for the R/L equivalence conclusion. Reverse-cell consequence: if (x,y) belongs to a small sink and (y,x) is relevant, the latter belongs to an SCC containing L_y×R_x; otherwise this entire rectangle is irrelevant (by bidirectional closure). For a small sink with projection sizes (p,q), the reverse rectangle has ≥pq cells, so at least 9 for (3,3), 6 for (2,3)/(3,2), 4 for (2,2). Different small sinks can charge the same reverse rectangle; no unjustified additive charging.

## 4. Counting and asymptotics
All other sinks have at least four cells. If s_2,s_3 count small sinks and q is total, then |U|≥2s_2+3s_3+4(q−s_2−s_3)=4q−2s_2−s_3. The orientation lemma gives 2s_2+3s_3≤|X||Y|≤floor(a²/4). Thus 4q≤N+floor(a²/4)−2s_3, using the last term if s_3 known; also s_3≤floor(|X||Y|/3)≤floor(a²/12). In particular triple sinks cannot cover more than a quarter of a² cells; if no two-cell sinks, q≤floor((N+floor(a²/4)/3)/4), with leading 13/48 (not a universal bound since two-cell sinks exist). 
The prior joint omission theorem applies because each relevant class that occurs reaches a sink representative reporting the same class. Hence max(0,q−d) queries suffice. The geometry alone lowers the leading upper coefficient from 1/3 to 5/16, a 6.25% relative reduction. At L=0 the proven lower coefficient 1/32 remains, ratio now 10; for the normalized limsup 1/32≤C_optimal≤5/16. This is not existence of a limit or tightness of either coefficient. The target 1/4 is not obtained.

## 5. Existence of actual three-cell sinks with fixed statistics
Construct a balanced family with sizes s=(1,2,2,4), group G=C3×C4, four disjoint blocks A_i={(u,c):u mod 3,c mod s_i}, B_i={v mod 3}, T_i={c mod s_i}, and absorbing zero. The carrier size is 12+39+10=61; K=union T_i plus zero is null. On each block A_i use rectangular band multiplication (u,c)(u',c')=(u',c); B_i is left-zero. Units (g,h) act on the left by shifting c of A_i,T_i by h and v of B_i by g; on the right shift u of A_i by g, fix B_i,T_i. For each i choose a three-bit mask m_i, and set A_(i,u,c) B_(i,v)=T_(i,c) if bit u+v mod 3 is 1, zero otherwise. A_(i,u,c) T_(i,c')=T_(i,c); T_i B_i fixes T_i; every remaining nonunit product zero. Cross-block products zero. Associativity follows from the same band/translation identities as the shared-target note, with mod 3 replacing mod 2, and also verified exhaustively below. The fixed products with T_i ensure principal ideals do not depend on masks; each proper principal ideal omits at least one of A_i,B_i for every i. D* invariant, including quotient, actions, idempotents and regularity.

Promise: precisely the masks for sizes {1,4} vanish, or precisely the masks for sizes {2,2} vanish; each other mask any nonzero number 1..7. 98 tables. A generating subset must generate G, meet all eight A_i/B_i blocks and meet both missing T layers; these conditions suffice by unit transitivity, witnesses for present T and zero products. For missing layers the count and size product are fixed (2 and 4); thus r and full minimal enumerator agree, and g_r=h_r. Here r=11, h_11=g_11=1679616, h_12=3359232, all other h zero. The only possible indispensable element is the unique T_0 member, indispensable exactly when m_0=0. This is a full T56 example for L=0 only, not all L. It can be extended to larger L by using the already-known balancing multisets and a group with a suitable second cyclic coordinate, but not executed here.

For the singleton layer A_0,B_0 each has three elements; A_0 is right-zero and B_0 left-zero. Every nonself factorization of an A_0 element uses a different A_0 element followed by a nontrivially shifting unit; every nonself factorization of B_0 uses a shifting unit followed by a different B_0 element. Thus the original left and right edges remain within each three-cell set {(u,v):u+v=delta mod 3}. The unit transfers reach both other cells. Their L-row and R-column classes are singleton, so augmentation leaves the three sinks intact. In fact fixing m_0=7 and flipping one chosen bit while another remains active and all other masks fixed changes exactly those three cells and nothing else in the full table. This rules out any exact unary dependency from one such cell to an outside cell over the family, even beyond the algebraic graph. It does not rule out joint/statistical shortcuts or assert a universal lower coefficient from these examples. Three-cell sinks truly occur with prescribed statistics; a universal minimum sink size four is false.
