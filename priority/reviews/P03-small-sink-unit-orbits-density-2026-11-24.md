# Exact small-sink unit orbits, weighted charging, and a dense-graph obstruction
24/11/2026. Internal continuation of [Green saturation](P03-three-cell-green-sink-bound-2026-11-24.md). No new T numbers, main modification, external referee or formal proof certificate. Historical priority unresolved.

## 1. Setting
Use precisely D* and the closed relevant-cell set U of the preceding notes, including full nonself left/right witness-induced edges and the additional reversible Green-translation edges. Each relevant cell has J-incomparable coordinates in A=M outside G and K; it is variable over the compatible family. Write a=|A|, N=a(a−1), q for the number of sink SCCs. Assume no exterior indispensable elements, since otherwise emptiness is immediate. The previous orientation lemma gives, for every sink of size 2 or 3, singleton L-classes on the first projection, singleton R-classes on the second, and R-equivalence of first coordinates and L-equivalence of second coordinates. Both projections have size at least two.

## 2. Classification: every small sink is an exact balanced unit orbit
For any relevant cell (x,y) and unit g, the balanced move
\[
(x,y)\cdot g=(xg,g^{-1}y)
\]
is an edge in the original witness-induced graph whenever it changes the cell. If xg differs from x, use the nonself factorization x=(xg)g^{-1}; its intermediate product g^{-1}y is outside K, so the left edge is exactly this move. Otherwise if g^{-1}y differs from y, use y=g(g^{-1}y) and the right edge. Applying the inverse unit gives the reverse edge. The product is unchanged by associativity; the move remains in U. These moves are present even before Green saturation.

Now let C be a sink of size 2 or 3 in the saturated graph. By the orientation lemma no nontrivial Green-saturation edges leave any of its cells (the relevant L- and R-classes are singleton). A left edge of the K-translation type from a nonself factorization x=uv would have first coordinate v different from x. Since x≤_L v and the first coordinates are J-equivalent, finite stability forces x L v, contrary to singleton L. Right K-translation edges are excluded dually. Hence a left edge is a transfer (x,y)→(u,vy), with x=uv and vy L y. If v were a nonunit then both x and vy lie in its proper two-sided principal ideal MvM; since vy J y, this ideal also contains y. But then xy is supplied by D*, contrary to variability. Thus v is a unit. Similarly every right transfer uses a unit middle factor. Here finiteness ensures MvM proper for nonunits. All outgoing edges within C are therefore balanced moves. Together with the preceding paragraph and closure of a sink, C is exactly one balanced unit orbit.

Projection onto each coordinate is a surjective equivariant map of transitive finite G-sets (the second action is y·g=g^{-1}y). All fibres have equal size, so each projection size divides |C|. As |C|=2 or 3 is prime and both projections have size at least 2, both are bijective. Thus all noninjective three-cell shapes (2,2),(2,3),(3,2) from the previous note are impossible in the saturated graph. The (3,3) shape exists by the previous balanced cyclic example. Both projection unit orbits have exactly |C| elements. Their stabilizers coincide under the equivariant bijection. In particular a triple sink entails an index-three subgroup of G, and 3 divides |G| (analogously 2 divides |G| for a double sink). This is not a classification of unsaturated sinks.

## 3. Joint weighted charging without double counting
Let X_i,Y_i be the first and second projections of all i-cell sinks, for i=2,3. These four sets are pairwise disjoint: X versus Y by the orientation lemma, and 2 versus 3 by the unit orbit sizes just proved. Each set is a union of full one-sided unit orbits. Write p=|X_2|, q2=|Y_2|, r=|X_3|, s=|Y_3|.

For any relevant cell in X_i × X_j, its SCC contains at least ij cells. Indeed the left unit action on its second coordinate is trivial (singleton L); balanced moves run over i first coordinates holding the second fixed, and Green saturation on the second coordinate gives at least j columns (its right-unit orbit lies in its R-class). Similarly Y_i × Y_j has at least ij by the dual argument, and Y_i × X_j has at least ij from the Green rectangle alone. For X_i × Y_j the balanced orbit has size divisible by both i and j, hence at least lcm(i,j); in particular mixed 2/3 pairs force six cells in either orientation, and the three same-size-3 non-forward blocks force nine. These are lower bounds on SCC size, not just sink size.

Assign a weight to each ordered off-diagonal cell: default 1/4; 1/2 on X_2×Y_2; 1/3 on X_3×Y_3; 1/6 on either ordered orientation between X_2 union Y_2 and X_3 union Y_3; 1/9 on X_3×X_3, Y_3×Y_3, Y_3×X_3. For each sink of size k every cell in it has weight at least 1/k by the preceding lower bounds and classification of all small sinks. Thus the sum of weights over a sink is at least one. Sink vertices are disjoint. Sum over all off-diagonal cells (including irrelevant cells) to get
\[
q\le \left\lfloor \frac{N}{4}+\frac{p q2}{4}+\frac{rs}{12}
-\frac{(p+q2)(r+s)}{6}
-\frac{5}{36}[r(r-1)+s(s-1)+rs]\right\rfloor.
\]
Equivalently the triple terms after combining rs are −(r²−r+s²−s)5/36−rs/18, hence never positive. With no double sinks, p=q2=0, so q≤floor(N/4), strictly stronger whenever triples occur and the penalty exceeds rounding. Under T56 statistics the same omission theorem gives max(0,q−d) queries; the conditional upper coefficient is now 1/4, instead of 13/48, where d=1+|J_L|. The instance bound can be minimized with previous bounds. Without the no-double condition, worst case still bounded by 5/16. The weights count cells once, including when several small sinks have the same reverse rectangle. No assumption of disjoint reverse rectangles is used.

## 4. Obstruction: sharp graph density in a, even at L=0
An explicit infinite family shows that a universal upper coefficient strictly below 5/16 for the *number of sinks of this graph in terms of a* is impossible, even with fixed rank, h, and g_r, and with both E-empty and E-nonempty inputs. This is not an algorithmic lower bound on the decision problem, and not a positive leading coefficient in n.

Take m right-zero blocks X_i and m left-zero blocks Y_i, each of size 2. Index all 2m blocks by i; attach a singleton t_ij to each ordered pair i≠j. Use G=C4×C4; its first coordinate shifts parity on the right of X blocks and left of Y blocks, all other main actions trivial. Within a block use its band product. Between distinct blocks i,j use t_ij or absorbing zero z: for X→Y two independent parity bits indexed by u+v mod 2, for all other orientations one bit constant across the block pair. Fixed actions are x_i t_ij=t_ij and t_ij y_j=t_ij for every member of the indicated blocks, and all other such products zero. All K-products are zero. Unit actions fix main t_ij.

Adjoin three orthogonal auxiliary components from the prior rectangular shared-target construction, with sizes s=2,2,4. Each has A=(u,c) in C2×C_s with rectangular band law (u,c)(u',c')=(u',c), B=C2 left-zero, T=C_s as target layer in K. Units shift A rows and T indices by their second coordinate, shift A parity on the right and B parity on the left by their first coordinate. Auxiliary AB gives T_c or z by either of two bits indexed by u+v mod 2; fixed AT=T_c and TB=T. Unlisted nonunit products, including across auxiliary components or main/auxiliary, are zero. Thus K consists of all main singleton targets, eight auxiliary layer members and z, and is null.

Associativity of main triples: if all factors belong to the same block use the band law; if first two blocks coincide, their product chooses the second parity when the following pair is X→Y, otherwise the cross product is constant, and left action on the target agrees; if last two coincide use the dual argument. If adjacent block indices are distinct, and neither can provide the required matching action on the other product, both bracketings give zero. For triples with a target factor the matching left/right actions compose as orthogonal projections; products with two K factors give zero. Auxiliary components satisfy the prior rectangular construction identities. Unit actions preserve these identities: main X→Y transfers parity from one coordinate to the other; all other cross products constant, and auxiliary unit transfers and translations are as in the earlier construction. Mixed orthogonal components remain zero. This proves associativity for all masks, not only the sample. Identity is the group identity.

All labelled principal ideals are fixed: within each main block the available products with its incoming/outgoing targets are already supplied by the fixed actions, and every proper principal ideal contains at most one exterior band block. Auxiliaries have the analogous fixed action argument. Therefore all proper principal internal tables, the quotient, K table, unit actions and K-actions are fixed. All exterior band elements are idempotent, all units regular, and the nonzero null targets are nonregular. The Green classes of the targets are their singleton or auxiliary layer. The full D* data agree.

Promise A: all main singleton layers present (every cross-block mask nonzero), both size-2 auxiliary masks vanish, size-4 auxiliary mask nonzero. Promise B: exactly one main singleton layer absent (its mask zero), every other main mask nonzero; both size-2 auxiliary masks nonzero, size-4 mask zero. In each present layer any nonzero mask is allowed independently. E is empty exactly in A. A generating set must generate G, meet every exterior band block (whose complement is closed), and meet each missing layer. Conversely these conditions suffice by unit transitivity and cross products for present layers; z is generated by K-products. Both promise types have exactly two missing layers of product 4, so the rank, full minimal enumerator and g_r coincide. Specifically G=C4×C4 has rank 2 and 48 inclusion-minimal generating subsets, all of size two (invertible ordered pairs modulo 4 are 96; unordered 48, or use spanning modulo 2). Hence r_gen=2m+10, h_r=g_r=196608·4^m; all other h_j=0. The result is at L=0, not all L.

Every main ordered cross-block cell is relevant (the all-present case permits every target and any one singleton can be absent); all auxiliary AB cells are relevant as well. Nonself decompositions of main exterior elements occur only by the shifting units, because within a right/left-zero block one factor must equal the product, and all other nonunit products fall in K. Between distinct blocks, original edges from a cell remain in that ordered block pair. X→Y balanced moves give two two-cell sinks; other orientations give one four-cell sink (independent row and column changes, or the Green rectangle). Green saturation cannot connect different block pairs. Auxiliary AB blocks give two sinks of 2s cells each (parity transfer and row translations); no cross-component relevant cells. Thus
\[
a=4m+22,\quad |K|=4m^2-2m+9,\quad n=4m^2+2m+47,
\]
\[
q=5m^2-2m+6,\quad \lim_{m\to\infty}q/a^2=5/16.
\]
This proves sharpness of the leading coefficient of the saturated graph count normalized by a, not of D_query. In fact decision in the full promised family costs exactly TWO deterministic queries: query the two parity positions of the size-4 auxiliary component, whose presence is equivalent to promise A. No one query suffices: on an auxiliary AB cell an active mask may yield zero at that particular bit while an absent mask also gives zero; on a main cross-block cell choose a different missing singleton for promise B and keep the queried mask full in both (there are at least two singleton targets); every other cell is fixed. This proof uses the full masks family, not just the samples tested below. Consequently q/n²→0 and actual decision complexity is constant on these families. The obstruction is to further purely graph-count charging in terms of a, not to a 1/4 bound for the real decision problem or for q normalized by n.

The same construction allows blocks of size 2 or 3 with G=C_lcm(4,block sizes)×C4, parity replaced by u+v mod gcd(block sizes) for X→Y, all other orientations constant. Each X→Y ordered block pair has gcd many sinks of size lcm, each other cross-block pair one SCC with size equal to the product. This follows from the independent row/column movements or balanced orbit as above. The auxiliaries and promise balancing still work (the common group factor need not have all minimal generating sets of size two). In particular mixed 2/3 cases and triple-only cases are explicit.
