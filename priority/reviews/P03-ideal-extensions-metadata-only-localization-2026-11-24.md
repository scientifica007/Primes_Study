## 2. Classical overlap, and an elementary translation-fibre observation

The canonical map tau(x)=(L_x,R_x) on an ideal K is classical (Grillet–Petrich Theorem 1.2). Their Theorem 4.1, under weak reductivity, reconstructs the extension from prescribed partial homomorphism and translations; the zero-quotient product in its last case is uniquely recovered from its inner bitranslation. Thus neither the canonical map nor this reconstruction principle is a novelty claim in P03.

In our labelled data, write q for the Rees quotient map. Every unknown product with q(x)q(y)=0 has its value in the known candidate set
\[
B_{xy}=K\cap xM\cap My\cap \tau^{-1}(\tau(x)\tau(y)).
\]
The equation and intersection are necessary, not sufficient for global compatible-table realizability. Singleton B determines the value without queries. In particular if K is weakly reductive (tau restricted to K injective), the entire table is known from quotient and K-factor products, even without ideals or generator statistics. This is an elementary application of the classical reconstruction principle.

If t in K is regular, choose an inverse b in K (replace an inner inverse u by utu), and e=tb, f=bt. For any z in K with tau(z)=tau(t), we have t=ez=zf. Hence J_t<=J_z. If J_t=J_z, stability yields t R z and t L z. In particular z=th for some h, so ez=eth=th=z, proving z=t. If z is regular as well, the symmetric argument gives J_z<=J_t; again z=t. Thus the fibre of a regular element has a unique regular member and every distinct member lies strictly above it in ambient J-order. This is an elementary consequence, not asserted historically new. A regular ideal is weakly reductive, but a nonregular ideal can also be weakly reductive; nonregularity alone is not sufficient for uncertainty.

## 3. Direct algorithm: remove enumeration of compatible tables from class planning

Use exactly D_red of the manuscript, with no need for any generating statistics in this section. All principal ideals and classes are supplied or derived. Let C be any nonregular J-class inside K, and U_C be all elements strictly above C. Presence means there is x,y in U_C with xy in C. The following plan is computed from metadata alone.

(A) If some pair x,y in U_C with at least one in K has its supplied product in C, declare present.

(B) If there exist t in C and x in U_C such that t belongs to xM but no r in C satisfies xr=t, declare present. Indeed xM supplies existence of some r with xr=t, and ideal monotonicity implies J_r>=C. Since equality has been excluded using known K-products, r lies strictly above C. Dually test t in My with no l in C satisfying ly=t, for y in U_C. If neither (A) nor (B) applies, every x in U_C with t in xM has a known factor r in C with xr=t, and likewise l in C with ly=t.

(C) Fix one c in C, define P_c={u:uc=c}, Q_c={v:cv=c}. If either intersects K or either has no nonunit, declare missing. Otherwise choose nonunit idempotents e in P_c and f in Q_c with minimal ambient J-class among their respective nonunits. They exist by positive idempotent powers and minimality. These stabilizers are submonoids contained in the exterior, and all their products and idempotents are known via the quotient. For each x in R(e) with c in xM and y in L(f) with c in My, retain the pair (x,y). Query one representative per distinct (L(x),R(y)) among retained pairs; declare present iff any response belongs to C. An empty retained set means missing. Choose representative deterministically by labels. Optional further safe filtering by the translation signature B_xy is not needed for the proof or the experiment.

**Proof of (C).** After (A) and (B) fail, any actual first-entry witness xy=t must have both coordinates exterior nonunits; units preserve J-class. By (B) choose r,l in C with xr=t=ly. If pt lies in C and px in K, either J_px>C, giving a supplied witness (px)y in C contrary to (A), or px in C, giving (px)r=pt in C contrary to nonregularity. Lower or incomparable J_px is impossible. Hence px is exterior nonunit. Dual on the right. This transports a witness to any c=ptq in C using (px)(yq). In particular a stabilizer element from K would contradict the exterior transport. Further r L c and l R c by stability, so r=uc and l=cv for some u,v; xu in P_c and vy in Q_c are nonunits. Thus any presence implies both stabilizer conditions in (C).

In the remaining case choose e,f as specified. Any first-entry witness xy=c transports to (ex)(yf)=c with ex R e and yf L f: exu in P_c is nonunit, J_exu<=J_ex<=J_e forces equality by minimality, then stability, and dually for yf. The new rectangle coordinates are regular exterior nonunits and satisfy the retained ideal conditions. Conversely any representative response in C is a first-entry witness since the representative coordinates lie in exterior J-classes above C (their principal ideals contain c and the class order is fixed). The output J-class is constant on L(x)×R(y) by mutual divisibility; hence the queried representative detects every localized witness.

**Size domination without a hypothetical witnessing table.** For any retained x in R(e) with c in xM, property (B) supplies r in C with xr=c, so r L c and r=uc, hence xu in P_c. For any d L c choose p,q with pc=d and qd=c. Then qpxu in P_c is nonunit, so J_qpxu<=J_px<=J_x=J_e gives equality. By stability px L x. The map d->px is injective because (px)r=d; this last identity uses a fixed K-product, not an unknown xy. Thus |L(x)|>=|L(c)|. Dual on the right gives |R(y)|>=|R(c)|. Consequently |L(x)||R(y)|>=|C| for every retained pair, whether or not any compatible table actually realizes xy=c. This is the key replacement for filtering on hypothetical compatible tables in the original manuscript.

With d selected classes of size at least s, the exact same weighted graph argument applies to the new rectangles and representatives: at most dB² small-rectangle queries, 2a²/B large-class queries, and at most d directed J-pairs in the three-region graph. Each edge satisfies the size domination, so the count is at most (a+sd)²/(4s)+dB²+2a²/B. Here a counts regular exterior nonunits, and d may count all selected classes, not just ambiguous classes. Since sd<=|K| this proves the same asymptotic bound for the selected-class algorithm and the same sharp 1/16 decision/count theorem when s=4. No enumeration of compatible tables is needed to determine which classes are ambiguous or which products are realizable; the plan may include harmless extra candidates. The plan uses only bounded loops over labelled elements/classes and ordinary finite set operations, hence polynomial-time planning of selected-class queries in explicit data.

## 4. Whole upper algorithm without enumerating extensions

The earlier general-ideal note already gives a direct signature algorithm for regular local generating families; use it explicitly here. Form the known image of the map x -> (q(x),tau(x)). Multiplication of signatures is given by the quotient and composition of translations; at most n distinct signatures arise since a compatible monoid exists. For a local subset S in a regular class C inside K, compute the closure of signatures of U_C union S. S is locally generating iff the closure contains (0,tau(t)) for every t in C. Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency take an idempotent e in C with et=t; a word of signature (0,tau(e)) evaluates to z in K and z²=e. A word of signature (0,tau(t)) then becomes t after left multiplication by e. Exterior local families use the nonzero quotient directly. This is the already-proved regular-invariance argument with no enumeration of compatible tables. Enumerate local subsets to compute the fixed generating polynomial factors r_*,g_* and E_*; this can still take exponential time in class size. For the nonregular composite classes in K, apply the direct plan in Section 3; the arithmetic formula then returns |E|. There is no claim of polynomial-time counting of minimum generating sets or of polynomial-time total runtime. The query bound remains unchanged.
