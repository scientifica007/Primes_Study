# P03: generator-class sweeps and sharp decision coefficient for sublinear ideals
24 November 2026. Internal mathematical development on scientific-priority branch only. No external literature retrieval, new T numbers, main modifications, or historical priority certification in this round. Earlier source-evidence correction in v1.11 remains controlling.

## 1. Results and scope

For L=4,5, every sequence of instances with |K|=o(n) admits exact nonadaptive deterministic emptiness decision using at most n²/40+o(n²) queries. There are opposite-verdict promised families with bounded (indeed fixed-size) ideals attaining n²/40-O(n) at every sufficiently large n. Thus the leading coefficient 1/40 is sharp in the sublinear-ideal regime, with either side-data model and either deterministic adaptivity regime. This is a regime statement, not a claim for every fixed prescribed ideal size or every sublinear upper envelope regardless of its constant. Cardinality still has sharp coefficient 1/36 even in bounded-ideal families (Theorem 12 and its one-row construction). The unrestricted decision coefficient is not determined. An improved explicit unrestricted bound is
\[
\limsup D_L(n)/n^2\le (1-1/(2\cdot10^8))^2/36.
\]
The strict separation of Theorem 13 is retained; its explicit gap is strengthened, but remains conservative.

## 2. Half-class generator lemma

Let M be a finite monoid with designated nonempty proper ideal K, and suppose no element of M\K is indispensable (otherwise emptiness can be decided immediately from the quotient). Let G be the group of units, A=M\(K union G). There exists S subset M\K containing G such that every exterior element is a positive word over S, and S meets at most |A|/2 nonunit L-classes. More precisely for each ambient nonunit exterior J-class I of size N, S meets at most N/2 L-classes, and if its L-class size l>=2, at most N/l. All choices are computable from the supplied quotient and labelled ideals, without queries.

Proof: process the exterior J-classes in descending order. For l>=2, include all members of I; there are N/l distinct L-classes. For l=1, for every x in I dispensability gives a nonself product x=uv with u,v distinct from x. Neither factor is in K. Both J-classes are >=I. If v J x, finite stability (v J uv implies v L uv) and the singleton L-class would give v=x, impossible. Hence J_v>I. Either J_u>I too (mark x seeded by higher classes), or u in I differs from x (add directed edge u->x, labelled by v, where J_v>I). Form the graph of all such factorizations using only quotient products. Every vertex is seeded or has an incoming edge from another vertex. In the condensation DAG, every source strongly connected component without a seed must have >=2 vertices: a singleton would have neither a seed nor an incoming edge. Choose one member of each such source component into S. At most floor(N/2) members are chosen. After higher elements have been generated, every marked vertex and chosen representative is available, and traversing graph edges generates every vertex of I. Every component of a finite DAG is reachable from a source, so no additional seeds are needed. This proves the induction and the bound, including singleton J-classes (which must be seeded from higher classes), and nonregular classes with singleton L. The dual yields a generator set T meeting at most |A|/2 nonunit R-classes, with per-class N/r bound if r>=2. The two generating sets need not be the same. This is a bound on the number of Green classes met, NOT on the cardinality or rank of S itself. For l>=2 we deliberately include all elements, not just one representative. Unit letters are never queried for K entry. 

## 3. Generator sweep for a canonical right endpoint

Use v1.4 metadata-only pretests and canonical endpoints. For any surviving target C, every first-entry product with output in C has exterior nonunit factors, and transport plus localization produces a right factor in the least right stabilizer class J_f which is a right multiple of the original right factor. In particular J_f<=J_y for every first-entry right factor y (not just the localized witness). This is the one-sided consequence of the existing transport/localization proof, not an assumption that every such witness lies in J_f.

Fix a regular exterior right endpoint J. For all surviving targets assigned to J on the right (any left endpoints), query one representative per pair consisting of a nonunit L-class met by S and an R-class in J, omitting supplied products. The number is at most sigma_L * |J|/r_J, where sigma_L is the number of nonunit L-classes met by S. This simultaneously decides presence of all those targets. To prove completeness take a localized witness xy in C with y in J and x exterior. Represent x by a positive word s_1...s_m in S, using quotient generation; all partial products giving x stay outside K because K is an ideal. Starting from y, prepend letters from right to left until the first product z in K. Let s u be this product, where u was the preceding exterior suffix value. Necessarily J_z>=C because the remaining left factors yield C. If J_z>C, some subsequent first entrance into C has a left exterior factor and a right K factor strictly above C, so that known product is a first-entry witness, contrary to the failed known-presence pretest. Thus z in C. Also J_u<=J by ideal monotonicity, and J<=J_u by the preceding one-sided consequence of localization. Hence u in J. The letter s is not a unit, since a unit cannot move an exterior element into K. The Green class-pair identity implies the queried representative of (L(s),R(u)) outputs an element of C. Conversely every reported C from the sweep is a genuine first-entry witness (two exterior factors), even if it was not in the original canonical rectangle. No need to know the actual suffix outputs: the sweep is a nonadaptive sufficient query set, not an algorithm that traces an unknown witness. All representative positions are metadata-defined. Dual sweeps apply to canonical left endpoints using T_R. 

## 4. Sharp main-term bound after removing small-region edges

Start with the two sufficient plans P8,P9 (both also query all sizes >=10). If known E_* is nonempty return immediately. As in v1.12, for integer B>9 remove all canonical pairs with first L-size or second R-size >=B, querying at cost <=2n²/B. Partition all regular exterior nonunits into X (r>=B,l<B), Y (l>=B,r<B), Z (l,r<B), and both-large. Denote their masses by the same letters; R=n-X-Y includes K, units, nonregular exterior and other regular classes. There are <=|K|/8 distinct selected edges because each edge is assigned a distinct target of size >=8. Hence incident Z mass is <=|K|B²/4. Paying for all remaining Z incident edges costs <=|K|nB²/16, using area>=8. Remaining edges X->Y. These are common preprocessing queries; counting overlaps is harmless.

Use b=mass of Y with r=9, d=mass of Y with r=1, C=mass of X with l=9, A_1=mass of X with l=1. The only common blocks of area below 10 are A_1×b and C×d. All other blocks cost at most 1/10 times endpoint mass product in the average of the two sufficient plans. Every block has average rate <=1/9 (common area>=9, single-plan area>=8 with half weight).

By the half-class generator lemma the total number of nonunit L-classes met by S is <=(X+R)/2+Y/B: the Y classes have l>=B and all other nonunit exterior elements contribute at most half their mass. Dually the total number of R-classes met by T is <=(Y+R)/2+X/B. These are bounds even when some nonregular classes are outside K; the lemma was not restricted to regular classes.

Right sweep option: replace ALL remaining canonical blocks with right endpoint in b by generator sweeps (not only A_1×b). They cost <=b((X+R)/2+Y/B)/9, simultaneously in both plans. The uncovered exceptional rectangle C×d can be queried in full at cost <=Cd/9. All other remaining blocks have average rate <=1/10. The average main cost is therefore at most
\[
(XY-Xb-Cd)/10 + b(X+R)/18 + bY/(9B)+Cd/9
= XY/10-2Xb/45+Rb/18+Cd/90+bY/(9B).
\]
The rectangles removed from the main sum are disjoint (b,d disjoint). All terms are valid upper bounds if some endpoint pairs are absent; sweeps need only handle assigned targets. The dual left sweep option exchanges X,Y, b,C, d,A_1, and costs at most
\[
XY/10-2YC/45+RC/18+A_1 b/90+CX/(9B).
\]

Choose the right option if Xb>=YC. Then Cd<=CY<=Xb, so its bound is <=XY/10+b(R/18-X/30)+n²/(9B). If R<=3X/5, this is <=n²/40+n²/(9B), since XY<=(X+Y)²/4. If R>3X/5, use the original uncompressed average bound XY/9 instead: n=X+Y+R>8X/5+Y implies XY<=5n²/32, so XY/9<=5n²/288<n²/40. When Xb<YC choose the dual left option, with the same proof using R<=3Y/5 or the raw average. Cases X=0 or Y=0 have zero main cost and are trivial. All choices are determined by metadata, including the cheaper of the two sufficient plans after any common sweep replacement; one can pay actual union size, which only reduces the bound. Thus a uniform decision upper bound at L=4,5 is
\[
D_L(n;\mathrm{metadata})\le
n²\left(1/40+19/(9B)+\frac{|K|}{n}\frac{B²}{16}\right).
\]
No asymptotic assumption was made in deriving this bound. It uses the original v1.12 small-region cost and new generator sweeps, not the previous grouped exit estimate. If |K|/n->0, choose B->infinity slowly enough that (|K|/n)B²->0 (e.g. B=max(10,floor((n/|K|)^(1/3)))). This yields n²/40+o(n²). The old 2x5 lower family with fixed target lists (v1.8 exact vector on 1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10: 7,-33,36,4,5,-36,22,-5) has fixed K, and gcd(2,5)=1 gives every sufficiently large order without padding. Therefore the upper coefficient is sharp on sequences allowing that bounded ideal family. For count the fixed ideal one-row U=1,V=9 lower family and global upper give 1/36 in the same sublinear regime. Both lower statements hold even with H supplied. The upper statements do not require H.

## 5. Improved unrestricted numerical gap

Combine this upper estimate with the v1.12 bound (n-|K|/2)²/36+O(n^(5/3)), uniform in metadata. Let B=1152, epsilon=10^-8. If |K|/n<=epsilon, the new bound has coefficient
\[
1/40+19/(9\cdot1152)+1152²/(16\cdot10^8)=0.02766200172839506\ldots
\]
which is strictly below gamma'=(1-1/(2*10^8))²/36, approximately 0.0277777775. Otherwise the refined surcharge gives coefficient <=gamma'+o(1). Hence the unrestricted limsup is <=gamma'<1/36. This replaces the earlier epsilon=2^-31 by 10^-8 and improves the explicit guaranteed gap by over 21 times. The magnitude of the improvement is small in absolute terms, not a practical estimate of the optimal unrestricted coefficient. The theorem at sublinear ideal size is the main improvement. We have not controlled the Z-incident cost sharply enough when |K| is a positive fraction of n. No general sharp decision coefficient or ordinary limit for unrestricted decision is asserted.
