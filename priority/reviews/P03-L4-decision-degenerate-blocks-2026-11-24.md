# P03: L=4,5 decision — isolate and compress degenerate blocks
24 November 2026. Scientific-priority branch. Internal mathematical proof and computational audit, no historical novelty certification, no main changes or new T numbers. The general L=4,5 decision gap is NOT closed. Cardinality remains settled by Theorem 12.

## 1. Two sufficient plans and the only low-area overlap

Use D_red (or D*) and L=4 or 5. If known E_* is nonempty, return nonempty without querying. Otherwise the exterior is E-free. The arithmetic matrix on sizes 1,...,9 has rank 8 and kernel spanned by (5,-26,36,-8,0,-18,0,20,-9), as proved/audited previously. After recovering all sizes >=10, either size 8 or size 9 fixes singleton multiplicity. Thus form two nonadaptive plans P8 and P9, each containing the canonical class-pair blocks of unresolved nonregular interior classes of size >=10, plus those of size 8 or 9 respectively. Use the metadata-only pretests of v1.4. One may choose the cheaper plan from supplied data. This is sufficient even for cardinality, but the special compression below uses E-free exterior, hence applies to decision (or cardinality on that restricted promise).

For an ordered endpoint class pair (i,j), set d_ij=l_i r_j and b_ij=N_i N_j/d_ij, the canonical number of representatives. By size domination a block serving size k has d_ij>=k, indeed l_i>=|L(c)| and r_j>=|R(c)|. A block in just one plan contributes half its cost to the average; a block in both contributes its full cost. All used blocks have d_ij>=8. Those with d_ij>=10 satisfy the required cost per mass product <=1/10 even without compression. A single-plan block of area 8 or 9 also satisfies it. A common block with d_ij=9 must serve sizes 8 AND 9, since a size >=10 cannot be served there. The factors of 9 give dimensions (1,9),(3,3),(9,1). The (3,3) case cannot serve size 8: the target sizes l_c,r_c are at most 3, and l_c r_c=8 h_c with h_c a positive integer, impossible (the only possible product <=9 would be 8, which is not a product of two positive integers <=3). Thus the only problematic blocks are the common degenerate 1x9 or 9x1 blocks. This refers to the endpoint Green-class dimensions, not to the geometric dimensions of the entire oracle table.

## 2. Weighted two-plan inequality

Here is the refinement of Lemma 6 required for averaging. Let X,Y,Z be disjoint vertex groups with positive dimensions l_i,r_i, nonnegative masses N_i and N_i<=l_i r_i in Z. Let F be a set of directed edges in X×Y, X×Z or Z×Y. Assume for every edge d_ij=l_i r_j>=8, and weights 0<=w_ij<=1 with 10 w_ij<=d_ij. Then
\[
\sum_{ij\in F} w_{ij}\frac{N_iN_j}{l_i r_j}
\le \frac{(\sum_i N_i+8|F|)^2}{40}.
\]
Proof: omit zero-weight edges or retain them harmlessly. As in Lemma 6 concentrate the X mass U at a maximum-linear-coefficient vertex x, then the Y mass V at y; the expression cannot decrease. If the central edge x->y is absent (including zero weight or missing group), the expression is at most (U+V) sum_Z N_i /10. Otherwise put lambda=l_x,rho=r_y with lambda rho>=8. The central edge contributes at most UV/10. Single-incident Z vertices contribute at most N max(U,V)/10. For a doubly incident Z vertex with dimensions l,r and mass N, let a=10 w_xz, b=10 w_zy. Then r>=a/lambda,l>=b/rho, so
\[
(l-b/\rho)(r-a/\lambda)\ge0
\]
gives a l/lambda+b r/rho <= lr+ab/(lambda rho). Multiply by N/(lr)<=1 to obtain
\[
\frac{aN}{\lambda r}+\frac{bN}{l\rho}
\le N+\frac{ab}{\lambda\rho}\le N+100/8\le N+16 .
\]
Charge 8 units to each of the two distinct incident edges of each doubly incident vertex. No such edge is charged twice across Z vertices, so total additions <=8|F|. With W the incident Z mass plus these additions, the expression is at most [UV+W max(U,V)]/10 <= (U+V+W)^2/40. This proves the inequality without assuming the synthetic masses are realizable semigroup classes.

Apply the same small-rectangle and large-class exclusions as Theorem 7 to the union of the two plans with B=max(2,ceil(n^(1/3))). Pay O(n^(5/3)) for those blocks (uncompressed counting suffices), since each selected target has size>=8, and each target contributes one canonical edge. The remaining edges lie in the same three regions. Assign one distinct target of size >=8 to every edge, which is possible because a target is assigned only one edge. Hence 8|F|<=|K| and sum N_i+8|F|<=n. If all remaining common area-9 blocks can be queried at cost at most 9b_ij/10, set w_ij equal to the corresponding cost fraction times 1 or 1/2 (according to plan membership). All weights satisfy the hypotheses, giving the upper bound n²/40+O(n^(5/3)). Exit queries from the local compression below may lie in other blocks: counting them against their source block overcounts the union but remains a valid upper bound. Use the actual union of query positions when choosing the cheaper plan; the two cost sums bound it.

More generally let kappa_ij be any metadata-computable upper cost for querying the relevant targets of a common degenerate area-9 block, at most b_ij by using the original block if cheaper. Use the same combined query plan for both alternatives when needed. Cap its weight at 9/10 in the numerical inequality, then add the excess. With the sum over all common degenerate area-9 blocks (including small ones, harmless overcount),
\[
D_L(n;\text{instance})\le n^2/40+
\sum_{\text{common degenerate area-9 blocks}}\max(0,\kappa_{ij}-0.9b_{ij})+O(n^{5/3}).
\]
This is an instance-dependent upper bound; no claim that the residual is universally o(n²). The trivial global 1/36 bound is still available.

## 3. Unit-orbit compression

The group of units acts on canonical pairs by (L(x),R(y)) -> (L(xg),R(g^{-1}y)). Green L is stable under right multiplication, Green R under left multiplication, so the action is well defined and bijective on the endpoint classes. All these permutations are known from the nonzero quotient. Product class is constant on each orbit by (xg)(g^{-1}y)=xy and the class-pair identity. Query one representative per orbit. Thus kappa can be taken as the number of orbits, with supplied cells optionally skipped. For l_i=1, if every x in J_i is moved by some right unit, each orbit has size>=2 (L(x) singleton); the dual holds for r_j=1 and left units. This yields at most b_ij/2, more than sufficient for the 0.9 threshold. A weaker sufficient test is simply to compute the orbit count <=0.9 b_ij.

**Restricted sharp theorem.** In the class of instances in which every common degenerate area-9 block satisfies that orbit-count condition (or the more general compressed-cost condition of Section 2), the worst-case deterministic decision cost has leading coefficient 1/40, for L=4,5 and both side-data models, adaptive and nonadaptive. Upper: Sections 1–3, plus immediate decision for nonempty known E_*. Lower: the previous 2x5 nondegenerate rectangular-band construction has area 10 for its canonical block (P,Q kernels are the two bands; their target actions yield exactly these classes), so the condition is vacuous; its all-order lower bound n²/40-O(n) remains valid in this restricted class. This is NOT a sharp theorem for unrestricted decision.

## 4. Local factorization compression beyond units

Suppose E has no exterior members. Consider a canonical block (I,J) with l_I=1, |I|=p>=2, and q=N_J/r_J. Assume the targets assigned to it survive the metadata-only known-presence tests. All elements of I form a right-zero band: finite regular Green incidence gives one R-class and singleton L/H-classes, each containing an idempotent; if a,b in I then b=a m and ab=a²m=b.

For each x in I, choose a known nonself factorization x=uv (exists by exterior dispensability, and all factors are exterior). Necessarily J_v>I: if J_v=I then stability gives v L x and v=x, a contradiction. Fix e in I distinct from x. Since ex=x, e u v=x, so J_eu=I. If eu differs from x, take a_x=eu,t_x=v. Otherwise take a_x=e,t_x=u; in this case u cannot lie in I since eu=u there. Thus in both cases
\[
x=a_x t_x,\quad a_x\in I,\quad a_x\ne x,\quad J_{t_x}>I .
\]
All choices can be made by scanning nonzero quotient entries; choose a unit t_x if possible. Let h be the number of distinct L-classes of the chosen nonunit t_x. No K element can be such a factor.

Let Y consist of one representative of every R-class in J. On the pq vertices (x,y) in I×Y, inspect the known quotient of t_x y, not its unknown value. There are three cases.

1. If t_x y is exterior and lies in J, map (x,y) to (a_x, representative_R(t_x y)) within the block. The product J-class is equal in every table by associativity and the Green class-pair identity. This edge changes its first coordinate, hence no self-loop.
2. If t_x y is exterior and lies strictly below J, mark the vertex irrelevant to every assigned target. Indeed if a_x(t_x y) lies in an assigned target C, both factors are strictly above C (exterior). Transport that first-entry witness to the chosen c of C using Lemma 4, then localize using the minimal right stabilizer of c as in Lemma 5. The resulting right coordinate is in J=J_f and is a right multiple of t_x y, forcing J<=J_(t_x y), a contradiction. The transport and localization are valid for surviving targets by the v1.4 direct-plan proof; no ambiguity enumeration needed.
3. If t_x y lies in K, assign an exit query at (representative_L(t_x),y). Here t_x is necessarily a nonunit. If xy lies in any assigned C, then t_x y lies in C: by ideal monotonicity it cannot be below/incomparable, and a strictly higher K output would make a_x(t_x y) a supplied first-entry witness, excluded by the known-presence test. Conversely an exit response in C is itself a first-entry witness since t_x and y lie strictly above C. The Green class-pair identity permits the representative at the exit. We recover presence, not the precise output of every source vertex.

Follow the functional graph of internal edges until reaching a marked irrelevant vertex, an exit or a directed cycle. Query one representative per directed cycle and every distinct exit. All cycles have >=2 vertices; a possible witness either leads to another equal-class witness in the block, or to an exit witness. Every queried response in an assigned C is a first-entry witness. Therefore this suffices to decide every assigned class simultaneously. With E_exit the number of distinct exits, it costs at most
\[
\frac{pq+E_{\rm exit}}2\le \frac{(p+h)q}{2},
\]
since each distinct exit has at least one source vertex, cycles are disjoint from exit vertices, and E_exit<=hq. Integer floors can be applied. Take the minimum with pq and with the orbit plan. For q defined as the number of R-classes, this can be used as kappa in Section 2. The dual holds for r_J=1. If p=1 the block may simply be queried (it is small in one dimension); no factorization claim is made in that case. The bound is local; summing h over many blocks may double-count higher elements, so no general resource bound for the residual follows from it alone.

## 5. Why the condition cannot be inferred from exterior dispensability

Two explicit examples use U=1,V=9 and p=4,q=2, layers sizes 8 and 9, with W either all zero or a single active position (unit case requires its partner).

(a) Use C4 units acting by swapping pairs of A labels on the right, trivially otherwise; W constant on each pair of A rows. Then associativity follows from the original one-row construction and invariance under that action. A labels are dispensable by units, B by row/column coverage, units by alternative generator choices. Both targets have the same canonical 1x9 block of 8 representatives, compressed to 4 by orbits/local cycles.

(b) Instead use C3 with trivial actions, and add a higher band H=[2]×[p]. Write h_(r,i)h_(s,j)=h_(r,j), h_(r,i)a_j=a_j, a_i h_(r,j)=a_j. Let H×B and H×T have precisely the same outputs as their projections a_i×B and a_i×T, with all other new nonunit products zero. Map H to A by column projection and fix the original monoid elsewhere. This is a homomorphism to the original one-row monoid, injective off H. A product can lie in H only when every nonunit letter is in H and at least one is present, where the band law and trivial unit action ensure associativity. Otherwise both bracketings project to the same label and are outside H, hence equal. All exterior labels are dispensable: H and B have both dimensions >=2, A_i=a_j h_(r,i) for j!=i, and C3 has alternative generators. Both target classes again have the same canonical 1x9 block (minimum left stabilizer class A, minimum right stabilizer class B). The unit orbits are now singletons. Local factorization queries exit to H×B and need 8 representatives in the tested cases. Thus the automatic orbit-compression hypothesis is false even with E-free exterior.

The second example extends to the complete L=4,5 balanced size lists of v1.9 with p,q sufficiently large (all 122 layers, 61 labels in each present list). The only variable cells are A×B and H×B with the same W. Fixed ideal descriptions follow from the original band actions: H principal ideals do not contain B, B principal ideals do not contain H or A; H×B outputs already available through H×T and T×B. The H-to-A projection sends columns surjectively, so all H and A principal ideals are independent of W. The exterior generating condition is a row/column cover of H and B, a nonempty subset of A (H never generates A without an A letter; once one A is available every A is generated via H), and a generating subset of C3. Its polynomials are fixed, and all target factors remain as before. Thus the obstruction can occur even with the full L=4,5 statistics and opposite emptiness verdicts. This extension is proved by the construction, not instantiated at full size in this round. Its independent W positions cost pq, but exterior size is 3p+9q+O(1), leading coefficient only 1/108; it does NOT improve the 1/40 lower bound. This exhibits the additional resource that the local compression bound needs to charge globally.
