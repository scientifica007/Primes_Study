## 2. Spot-Checkers and the exact versus approximate distinction

The source distinguishes a property check with a positive distance gap from exact recognition. Its Section 4.4 establishes quadratic-order exact commutativity and joint associativity/commutativity lower bounds (Theorems 53 and 54 of the 1999 manuscript), using uninspected blocks and preserving cancellativity in the harder examples. It applies Yao's principle for randomized lower bounds. P03 cannot claim priority for exact algebraic property decision being quadratically hard, local table perturbation, or the exact/approximate distinction. However P03 promises associativity on BOTH sides, with common labelled ideals, quotient, K-products and generating statistics, and asks the irreducible-element predicate. Neither source theorem implies the sharp P03 coefficient. We did not read every section of this 30-page paper, so the no-match statement is limited to inspected material.

## 3. Direct precedence: indispensable/irreducible elements

Schmid Section 3, p.409, defines operation-wise irreducibility by the same fundamental-operation criterion and relates it to the complement of a singleton being a maximal subalgebra (Fact 3.1). Proposition 3.3 gives equivalences for generation by irreducibles, fixed maximal subalgebras, and the genuine generators. Schmid cites A. Pasini, On the Frattini subalgebra of an algebra, Boll. Un. Mat. Ital. (4) 12 (1975), 37–40, as an earlier source [18]. Pasini's text has not been read here; the 1975 priority lead is not yet directly verified.

Bergman–Slutzki Proposition 3.8 explicitly states the equivalence of irreducibility, closure of the complement, and the condition on each fundamental operation. For a single binary multiplication operation and no nullary constant this specializes exactly to Lemma 2 of the P03 manuscript. Proposition 3.9 proves deterministic log-space membership testing given the full finite operation tables. This is computational space complexity rather than table-query complexity. Their NP/coNP results for relative generators/non-generators concern different predicates; do not identify the complement of E with the Frattini subuniverse. Lipparini Section 2 likewise explicitly defines indispensable elements. The underlying definition and two-factor criterion are classical, not independently original. P03 uses the binary reduct of a monoid; a signature with identity as a nullary operation would make the identity freely generated and change the predicate. Credit the cited authors directly in Lemma 2. The project name does not confer novelty.

## 4. Direct precedence: nonregular class status

For a nonregular J-class C, DMW Lemma 3.2 gives the alternative between generating the full semigroup after removing C and the complement being a maximal subsemigroup. Proposition 3.3 tests the second alternative by generation from higher-class generators. Taking the generating set to be the entire semigroup makes their condition precisely the P03 definition of a missing class. Corollary 3.4 identifies whether an irredundant generating set meets C. Proposition 1.4(a) attributes the structural classification to Graham, Graham and Rhodes (1968); only the abstract of the earlier paper was inspected this round.

Thus the compulsory/noncompulsory class distinction is established in the literature. The local-family enumerator (1+w)^|C|-1 versus (1+w)^|C| is an elementary counting consequence of the dichotomy and Green-class generation; do not present it as an independent discovery. Gray's work supplies classical rank and relative-rank methods; it does not imply, from the parts inspected, the invariance of labelled regular local families under the P03 metadata, the prime-factor-count inference from r and g_r, or the selected-class query bound. These specific claims remain the narrower priority targets. Absence from the inspected excerpts is not a proof of novelty.

## 5. Exact budget and gap testing on the P03 balanced promise

This is a mathematical application motivated by the exact/gap distinction, not a theorem attributed to Spot-Checkers. Keep the L=0 split construction with U=V=2 from the sharp manuscript. Let m denote the number of free label positions (pq in the even construction, pq-1 in the odd padded construction). Each active position has one of two distinct labels from one of two disjoint lists; both labels of the chosen list must occur. All other free positions are zero. Let k>=2 be the number of active positions. Each label change alters exactly four multiplication cells, all in its own 2-by-2 rectangle.

**Promise-relative distance.** The minimum unnormalized Hamming distance from a promised table W to an opposite-verdict promised table is 4k. Every active position of W must change since the opposite list has disjoint labels. Conversely, assign the two opposite labels on exactly the k active positions, both at least once, and keep the zero positions and any fixed padding slot. Thus the distance is exactly 4k. The minimum opposite distance in this promise is 8, attained with k=2. Normalized by n² it tends to zero along the sparse lower-bound examples. This is a promise-relative distance, NOT an assertion of the distance to every compatible opposite table outside that subfamily.

**Gap test.** On this promise, accept empty E inputs and reject nonempty E inputs at normalized distance at least epsilon>0 from the empty class of the promise. Sample q independent uniform free positions with replacement. A nonzero answer reveals the case, and if no nonzero answer occurs, default to empty. This accepts every empty input with certainty. On a far nonempty input 4k>=epsilon*n², so k/m>=epsilon since 4m<=n². The probability of missing every active position is (1-k/m)^q<=exp(-epsilon*q). Thus q=ceil(log(1/eta)/epsilon) suffices for error <=eta, or query all m if cheaper. This is a one-sided tester on the balanced promise; it is NOT a general P03 property tester, and nearby opposite inputs need not be rejected.

**Exact decision with fixed query budget.** For integers 0<=q<=m the minimax worst-case error probability for a randomized algorithm using at most q queries on every run is
\[
e_q=\frac12\frac{\binom{m-q}{2}}{\binom{m}{2}}.
\]
Upper: sample q distinct positions uniformly, classify on a nonzero answer, otherwise toss a fair coin. The largest miss probability occurs at k=2. Lower: take an equiprobable verdict, exactly two marked positions uniformly chosen, and a uniformly random bijection of that list's labels to the two positions. For each deterministic algorithm the all-zero transcript fixes a sequence of at most q distinct inspected positions, since fixed queries do not help. Conditional on missing both marks the verdict remains equiprobable, and the probability of that event is at least the displayed ratio. Therefore its average error is at least e_q; averaging over the coins gives the same lower bound for randomized algorithms. This differs from the earlier expected-query-cost result, which permits randomized stopping with a bound on expected cost. The exact fixed-budget formula holds only for the promise. For 0<eta<1/2, as m tends to infinity the smallest budget for error <=eta is (1-sqrt(2eta))m+O(1). This implies the corresponding global worst-case fixed-budget randomized lower coefficient at least (1-sqrt(2eta))/16 via balanced p,q; no matching global upper asserted.
