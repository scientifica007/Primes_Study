## 1. Scope and a general structural observation
Use D* of the preceding notes, including labelled principal ideals, unit actions, proper principal ideal tables, a designated proper ideal K, its full Rees quotient and all products involving K. Generation uses positive nonempty words. E is the set of elements without a factorization by two elements different from themselves. Computation is free. The optimal coefficient for emptiness with all T56 statistics remains open.
Any ambiguous indispensable target t is nonregular and its Green J-class is a singleton. Indeed all products outside K or with a factor in K or a unit are fixed. If a variable xy=t in one table while t is indispensable in another, the second table has xy=z≠t in K, with the same left/right translations as t on K, by associativity and fixed actions. If t is idempotent, z²=t²=t contradicts indispensability. If t is regular but not idempotent in the indispensable table, choose b with tbt=t and put e=tb∈K. Then e²=e, e≠t, et=t and ez=t by translation equality, again a contradiction. Regularity is common in the model. A nonregular J-class in a finite semigroup has no product of two members remaining in that class. If it has two different elements x,t, express t=axb using multipliers in the monoid. Each nonidentity multiplier must lie strictly above J (otherwise a product of two J-members would remain in J); all intermediate factors stay in J. This yields an alternative factorization of t: if ax≠t, use (ax)b with b≠t, and if ax=t use ax with a,x≠t. Thus a nonregular class of size >1 cannot contain an indispensable element. This observation does not assert that the local generating counts of regular classes in an arbitrary K are fixed.

## 2. Null-ideal factorization
For the rest of Sections 2–5 assume K is a null semigroup: all products inside K equal a fixed z∈K. Associativity forces z to be a global zero, since mz=m(uv)=(mu)v=z and similarly zm=z for u,v∈K. If |K|=1 the table is already known. Otherwise z is dispensable (square of any other member of K), and every nonzero member of K is nonregular. All J-classes contained in K, apart from {z}, are nonregular. The quotient gives the entire labelled generating family on M\K for the task of generating all elements outside K (ignore quotient zero). Let G_0(w) and H_0(w) enumerate such subsets by size, respectively all and inclusion-minimal; r_0 is their least size. These polynomials are known without queries, because products falling into an ideal never return to its complement. Exterior membership in E is also known. 
For each nonzero class J⊂K, call it missing if no product of two elements of strictly higher J-classes belongs to J. Equivalently the semigroup generated by all strictly higher elements does not meet J: in a binary evaluation tree first entry into J must have two strictly higher inputs, by ideal monotonicity. If one member of J is available, every member can be generated using it and the strictly higher elements, by the nonregular-class argument in T61/T69. Descending induction on the finite J-poset proves that a subset generates M iff its exterior part generates all of M\K and it meets each missing J-class; nonmissing classes impose no constraint, and z is then generated as a square. Conversely necessity follows because a word producing a missing J must have some input in J. Inclusion-minimal generating subsets consequently choose exactly one representative per missing class and an inclusion-minimal exterior choice. 
If Z is the collection of missing classes, s_J=|J|, b=|Z|, and P=prod_(J∈Z)s_J, then
\[
r=r_0+b,\qquad H(w)=H_0(w)P w^b,\qquad
G(w)=G_0(w)(1+w)^{|K|}\prod_{J\in Z}R_{s_J}(w),
\quad R_s(w)=1-(1+w)^{-s}.
\]
The ordinary generating polynomial G has coefficients g_j, and H has coefficients h_j. Thus b and P are available from r and h_r (indeed from g_r, since all size-r generating sets are inclusion-minimal). If exterior E is empty, |E| is exactly the number of missing singleton classes. This includes all previous shared-target families. Analogous formulas hold if other varying contributions are only from nonregular classes and the contributions from all other classes are known; no such assumption is made for general nonnull ideals.

## 3. Exact information equivalence: necessary as well as sufficient
For fixed L≥0 put J_L={j:1≤j≤L, j=1 or j even}. The complete information in r,H,g_r,...,g_(r+L), as far as the missing-size multiset is concerned under common exterior polynomials and common |K|, is precisely
\[
b,\quad P,\quad p_j=\sum_{J\in Z}s_J^j\ (j\in J_L).
\]
Equality of these quantities is equivalent to equality of the indicated statistics. Proof: the lowest term of the known factor G_0(w)(1+w)^|K| has degree r_0 with nonzero coefficient, so divide it out formally after removing w^r_0. From b and P, normalize the remaining product to constant 1. Use the invertible substitution x=log(1+w); after dividing by x^bP, the normalized product has logarithm
\[
-\frac{x}{2}p_1+\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{B_{2j}}{2j(2j)!}p_{2j}x^{2j}.
\]
This follows by differentiating log(sinh(x/2)/(x/2)) and using the Bernoulli generating function x/(exp(x)-1). Each B_(2j) is nonzero by Euler's formula for ζ(2j)>0. Hence the coefficients through L recover exactly the listed moments; all odd degrees ≥3 in this logarithm vanish. The previous sufficient balancing lemma is now an equivalence. Use rational arithmetic; no approximate logarithms of integers are necessary. H contributes no further information about missing sizes beyond b,P in this model. Full G determines |E| even without null K by T53.

## 4. Zero-query criteria and joint querying
Let d_1,...,d_h be the distinct sizes of the nonzero classes inside K, or the sizes of classes with unresolved status after subtracting all known contributions. Let m_i be the number of missing classes of size d_i. Build the exact integer matrix M with columns indexed by d_i and rows:
- 1 (number of classes);
- v_p(d_i) for each prime dividing any d_i (product P, converted to prime valuations);
- d_i^j for j∈J_L (moments).
The given statistics determine the right-hand side v of M m=v. Add bounds 0≤m_i≤number of available classes of size d_i. If size 1 is not present, E is already known from exterior tests; otherwise denote its column by i_1. If e_(i_1) lies in the rational row space of M, m_(i_1) is known and emptiness requires zero further queries. In particular if M has full column rank, all missing multiplicities are known, although the identities of the individual classes need not be known. Even without the row-space condition, integer feasibility may force m_(i_1)=0 or >0; if all bounded integer solutions have the same status, that is a sufficient zero-query test. Bounded integer solutions may be a strict superset of realizable patterns, so failure of that test is not a lower bound.
A uniform sufficient condition independent of the actual positive sizes is
\[
h\le 2+|J_L|.
\]
For h=1 use the count, for h≥2 consider 1, log s and enough of the powers s^j in increasing order to make h functions. The log row is a real linear combination of the prime-valuation rows. A nonzero combination with h distinct positive zeros would by Rolle have derivative with at least h−1 distinct positive zeros. After multiplying the derivative by s, it is a polynomial with at most h−1 nonzero terms, which has at most h−2 distinct positive zeros (Descartes' rule). If the derivative is zero identically, the combination is constant and cannot have those zeros unless all coefficients vanish. Thus the h×h evaluation matrix is nonsingular, and the integer matrix has full rank over Q as well. In particular h≤2 needs L=0, h=3 needs L=1, h=4 needs L=2, h=5 needs L=4; for h≥3 L≥max(1,2h−6) suffices. This is sharp as a uniform bound on support size for each L: the reduced polynomial of the preceding T56 construction has degree 2+|J_L|, all its coefficients are nonzero with alternating signs, and its positive/negative multisets of powers of 2 give a collision containing size 1 on one side only, on exactly 3+|J_L| available sizes. Their shared-target realization has E empty versus nonempty. For particular sizes the prime-valuation rows can give more rank than the uniform criterion (e.g. 1,2,3 already resolve at L=0).
If M is rank-deficient, choose a column basis B containing the size-1 column (nonzero because of the count row). Determine the missing multiplicities for sizes outside B by querying cells that can yield a member of such a class from strictly higher factors. Class status is obtained from the known entries plus those queries, by the first-entry criterion in Section 2. The remaining counts are then uniquely determined by M_B m_B=v−M_notB m_notB. This is a genuine joint inference rule; one need never query a cell merely because it is a possible witness for a singleton class. More generally choose a subset of sizes such that the singleton coordinate is determined in the remaining linear system, and optimize the size of the union of relevant cells. Adaptive bounded-integer feasibility can stop earlier. For instance when possible missing multisets are {1,4} versus {2,2}, knowing the missing count at size 4 determines the singleton count, even if no singleton witness was queried. The query cost is at most the cardinality of the union of still-unknown cells required by the selected size groups (known quotient/actions skipped); no new universal numerical upper coefficient for all D* is derived here. The 17/48 coefficient of the preceding note remains the current universal bound.

## 5. Composite moduli improve the lower construction
The previous shared-target multiplication proof needs only s_i | S for each target layer, not that S be a power of 2. Keep G=C4×C_S and the same row/column/parity action formulas; projection C_S→C_(s_i) is a group homomorphism whenever s_i divides S. Every associativity check, principal-ideal description and E calculation in the previous note is unchanged. The unit generating polynomial U_G and its inclusion-minimal counterpart H_G are common; one must NOT use the power-of-two formula 3S² for the number of minimal unit generating pairs when S has odd factors. Write r_G=rank(G) under positive generation. Then
\[
n=2Sp+2q+4S+D+1,\quad r=r_G+p+q+b,\qquad
H(w)=H_G(w)(2S)^p 2^q P w^{p+q+b}.
\]
The earlier ordinary enumerator and its moment balancing remain valid. r_G=2 for even S, 1 for odd S (S≥2), but we need only its constancy. For two complementary missing-label patterns of equal b, every active label appears and there are m=2pq positions with m≥b. The exact deterministic cost m−b+1 and optimal randomized expected cost (1−2ε)(m+1)/(b+1) follow verbatim from the previous shared-target query proof. At q=Sp the leading coefficient is 1/(8S), for fixed L and fixed chosen size multiset. 
Therefore we may minimize S by rational linear algebra. Form the matrix of Section 4 on all positive divisors of S. A signed integer balancing vector a with a_1≠0 exists iff the rational kernel contains such a vector (equivalently the size-1 column is in the span of all other columns). Scale to integers and reverse sign if needed to get a_1>0. Positive and negative entries define disjoint multisets of layers; the rows guarantee equality of b, P and all required moments. Only the positive multiset contains size 1. This condition is necessary and sufficient for this specific balancing scheme, not all possible semigroups. Search S=2,3,... up to the prior power-of-two upper bound 2^(2+|J_L|). Exact computation produces:

Least S means least among integer common moduli supporting a signed relation with nonzero singleton coefficient, verified by rational nullspace calculations for every smaller S, not minimal n, C or D. The relation chosen need not minimize C/D. For large L the additive constants may be much worse than the old scheme; improvements refer to leading coefficients as p,q grow for fixed L. At L=0, S=4 remains minimal in this scheme and the previous leading lower coefficient 1/32 is unchanged. No overall optimum proved.

Concrete L=2,3 example:
\[
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,a_6,a_{12})=(6,-21,10,15,-11,1).
\]
Positive sizes: 1 six times, 3 ten times, 4 fifteen times, 12 once. Negative sizes: 2 twenty-one times, 6 eleven times. Both cardinalities 32; both products 2^32 3^11; both first moments 108; both second moments 480. E has 6 members in the positive-missing pattern and is empty in the negative-missing pattern. S=12, D=216, b=32. Leading coefficient 1/96 versus previous 1/128. At L=6 or 7 coefficient 1/192 versus 1/512; at L=8 or 9, 1/288 versus 1/1024; at L=10, 1/384 versus 1/2048. No improvement in the L=0 global gap and no new general upper coefficient.
