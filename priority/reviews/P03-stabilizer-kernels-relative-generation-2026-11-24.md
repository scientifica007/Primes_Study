## 1. Elementary kernel observation

Let P be any submonoid of a finite monoid M. Write J for ambient Green relation. Multiply all elements of P in any order to obtain w. Then w lies in P and J_w <= J_v for every v in P. Hence P meets a unique least ambient J-class J_min. The same holds if we instead multiply all nonunits of P when this set is nonempty. Positive powers remain in J_min, since they lie in P and are <=J w. An idempotent power gives a deterministic choice in J_min. These are elementary finite-semigroup facts, not independent historical originality claims.

In fact P intersect J_min is exactly the minimal two-sided ideal of P (its kernel). To prove this let I=P w P. For every v in P, J_v >= J_min, but we must show the claim about ideals internal to P, not merely ambient ideals. Each a in P intersect J_min has a^2 J a; finite stability gives a H a², so a lies in a subgroup of M with identity a^omega. The product awa lies in I and in the same ambient J-class as a, and is both R- and L-equivalent to a by stability. Thus it belongs to the group H_a, so (awa)^omega=a^omega belongs to I. It follows a=a^omega a belongs to I. Conversely I is contained in P intersect J_min by minimality. For any v in P, ava with a in J_min belongs to PvP and H_a, so the identical argument shows a in PvP. Thus I is contained in every nonempty two-sided ideal of P. This proves the assertion. The kernel may have multiple idempotents or multiple ambient L/R classes; no uniqueness of an idempotent is claimed.

## 2. Canonical ordered class pairs in P03

Use the v1.4 metadata-only plan. After the known-product and ideal-inference tests fail, exclude C when either point stabilizer P_c,Q_c meets K or lacks a nonunit. Otherwise both stabilizers lie outside K and contain nonunits. Their multiplication is known from the quotient (closure in the exterior). By Section 1 their least ambient J-classes I_c,J_c are unique regular exterior classes. They depend on the chosen target c; we fix a deterministic representative c of each target class C. No claim of independence of c.

Choose any idempotents e,f in the respective kernels. For all x R e, xM=eM contains c because ec=c. For all y L f, My=Mf contains c because cf=c. Hence the ideal tests in the v1.4 retained-rectangle definition are automatic. Every L-class in I_c meets R(e), and every R-class in J_c meets L(f), by finite D=J and the rectangular Green incidence property. Therefore the pairs of L/R classes represented by the full rectangle R(e) × L(f) are exactly
\[
\{\mathcal L(x):x\in I_c\}\ \times\ \{\mathcal R(y):y\in J_c\}.
\]
The v1.4 size domination applies to all these pairs. One representative per pair decides presence, since output J-class is constant on each L×R block, and both endpoints are strictly above C. This plan does not depend on the idempotents chosen within the two kernels. It is not necessary to construct the rectangle at all.

For the set of selected classes not resolved in preprocessing, collect the distinct ordered pairs of least ambient stabilizer classes into a set of directed edges F. Let N_i=|I_i|, l_i=|L(x)| and r_i=|R(x)| for x in I_i. Each edge (i,j) contributes exactly
\[
b_{ij}=(N_i/l_i)(N_j/r_j)
\]
canonical query representatives. Different edges have disjoint blocks, so the number of representatives in the unpruned union equals sum over F of b_ij. Known products can be skipped; the number of actual new queries is at most this sum. Several targets assigned to the same edge share the entire query block. This is a count for this specific algorithm, not a proof of instance-optimality. It clarifies the graph in the existing weighted bound and retains the same universal 1/16 coefficient. It does not replace the small-rectangle / large-class / three-region argument with a stronger universal bound.
