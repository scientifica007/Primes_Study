## 3. Explicit one-sided and two-sided surjections

Let M be a finite monoid, c any element. Put P={u:uc=c}, Q={v:cv=c}. Choose idempotents e,f in their respective unique least ambient J-classes (the product of all members gives a least class, and an idempotent power remains there). The following elementary consequence of finite Green stability is independent of the ideal, any presence witness, regularity of c, and ambiguity:
\[
L(e)\longrightarrow L(c),\quad x\mapsto xc;\qquad
R(f)\longrightarrow R(c),\quad y\mapsto cy
\]
are surjective. Indeed x L e implies x=pe,e=qx, so xc=pc,c=qxc, showing the map is well defined. For d L c choose p,q with pc=d,qd=c. Then qpe belongs to P, since qpec=qpc=c. Thus J_e<=J_qpe<=J_pe<=J_e; stability implies pe L e, and (pe)c=d. Dual proof for R(f).

Moreover L(e)×R(f) -> J(c), (x,y)->xcy is surjective. For x L e and y R f choose k,l with kx=e,yl=f; then k(xcy)l=c, so xcy J c. For d J c write d=pcq. Since pc and cq lie in J(c) by ideal monotonicity, stability gives pc L c and cq R c. Choose x L e with xc=pc and y R f with cy=cq using the one-sided maps. To avoid a common incorrect step: xc=pc and cy=cq are sufficient because xcy=pcy=pcq. Thus d is obtained. Consequently |L(c)|<=|L(e)|, |R(c)|<=|R(f)|, |J(c)|<=|L(e)||R(f)|. The regular Green incidence and cardinal identity give the stronger existing |L(e)||R(f)|>=|J(c)||H(c)|. For c in K all maps use supplied K-factor products; the e,f choices are known whenever stabilizers avoid K (also in general their least ambient classes and idempotents are known from metadata). This provides an alternative proof of the size-domination step in Lemma 5 and its direct implementation, leaving exterior transport, presence equivalence, and the graph/arithmetic estimates unchanged. Do not claim historical novelty for this elementary lemma: it is directly motivated by classical stability and Pin's related approach. It does not by itself establish an oracle lower or upper bound beyond the prior results.
